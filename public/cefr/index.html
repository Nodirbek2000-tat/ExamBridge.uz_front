CEFR Levels

Six Levels, One Clear Path

The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) is the international standard used by schools, universities and employers worldwide to describe language ability.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the CEFR scale?
The Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR) is the international standard for language ability. It has six levels: A1 (Beginner), A2 (Elementary), B1 (Intermediate), B2 (Upper Intermediate), C1 (Advanced) and C2 (Mastery).
What CEFR level do I need for university?
Most European and international universities require B2 or C1 for undergraduate admission. Competitive English-taught master's programs may require C1 or C2. Always check your specific university's language requirements.
How long does a CEFR test take?
A full CEFR test includes Reading (45–60 min), Listening (30–40 min), Writing (45–60 min) and Speaking (10–15 min). You can also practice individual skills without completing the full test.
